PROJECT OMEGA: THE ROCKVILLE SYNDICATE
Chapter: Maria Brooke
Creative Brief — Final

LOGLINE

Eric spends weeks catfishing Ben through a fake account named Maria Brooke while the entire group watches and cheers in real time — and when the fiction collapses and Ben finds out who built it, his anger lands on Nick F and Jordan anyway.

LOCATION AND WHO'S PRESENT

The group chat, primarily. Ben's DMs with Maria Brooke running parallel. One track practice, in person, where Sean hears the thing that ends it. 2022.
Present: Eric, Jordan, Nick F, Nick H, Maharko, Sean. Target: Ben.

EMOTIONAL ARC

Start: The player feels like they're in on something good. The group is warm, the bit is landing, there's genuine pleasure in the shared joke. This is what being inside the circle feels like.
Turn: Ben tells Sean he's skipping track practice to go watch a volleyball game. Something real just got touched. The fiction has weight now.
End: Ben comes back. He's not mad at Eric. The group is glad to see him. The bit is still funny. The player is left holding the distance between what just happened and how everyone is acting about it.

CHAPTER IDENTITY

A nature documentary where the subject is also a member of the film crew, and doesn't know he's in the episode.

MARIA BROOKE — WHO SHE IS

Eric built her from the chatlog up. She's casual, warm, slightly self-deprecating, never too eager. She opens with a pretext — a survey about drugs and STDs for a human studies class — and withdraws it immediately, which makes her feel less like a setup. She deflects Snap gracefully, apologetically. She mirrors Ben's world without being obvious: she has a friend at RM, she's getting into F1, she plays varsity volleyball, she invites him to a game. When she finds out Ben has a track meet at WJ, she says "I didn't get to see you :(" — which is the moment that does the most damage. Eric didn't build a fantasy. He built someone real enough to rearrange a person's schedule for.

KEY DRAMATIC MOMENTS

Eric sends the first message as Maria Brooke — the account comes to life
The group chat assembles as an audience — everyone's in, nobody hesitates
"u got snap?" — Ben tries to escalate, gets gently shut down; the group's investment peaks
The kart racing walk-back — Ben brags about karts to impress a girl, catches himself mid-sentence, types "oh u meant track lmao" and undersells himself in the same breath. The group isn't just laughing at the slip. They're laughing because it confirms what they already knew: Ben is bitchless, and he just proved it to a girl he was trying to impress
Ben sends a photo of himself to Maria Brooke — to Eric. The group sees it. Whatever they say about it, they're saying it about Ben's face. The fiction has taken something real and made it available to everyone in the room
Ben tells Sean he's skipping track practice to go watch her play volleyball — the bit touches real life
Sean tells Ben it's a catfish — the fiction collapses, but Eric's name isn't necessarily in the room yet
Ben finds out it was Eric. His anger lands on Nick F and Jordan anyway. The group is genuinely glad to have him back


THE PLAYER'S POSITION

The player is in the group chat from beat one. They can see Maria Brooke's messages and the group's reactions simultaneously. They are, from the first moment, complicit. They didn't build the account. They're watching, and laughing, and that's enough. They have information Ben doesn't. The question of what to do with it is theirs alone.

THE CHOICE

Ben tells Sean he's going to skip track practice for Maria Brooke. The player has been in the group chat the whole time. They know everything.
Say something now — before Sean does — or stay quiet and let it play out.
Saying something costs: breaking the group's unspoken agreement, killing the bit, marking yourself as the one who looked away first. You were in the chat. You laughed. Saying something now means admitting that.
Staying quiet costs: you were there the whole time and said nothing. You're not different from anyone else. Maybe that's fine. Maybe that's exactly who you are in this group.
Neither option saves Ben. The choice is only about you.

THE BOSS

Not Eric. The group chat — the collective, unanimous decision that kept the bit going. No one person made it. Everyone made it, including Sean, who was laughing right up until it cost something in his world too. The player is fighting their own complicity.
Winning feels like: saying one true thing when it mattered, even knowing it wouldn't change the outcome.
Losing feels like: realizing you were laughing the whole time, and the laugh track was you.

SEAN — THE HINGE

Sean is peripheral — part of Ben's track world, getting closer to the Rockville group during this period. He was in the chat. He was laughing. He didn't have a moral awakening. He told Ben because Ben was about to skip their shared real obligation for a person who didn't exist — and that consequence lived in Sean's world, not just Ben's. His intervention wasn't ethical. It was practical. He's not a hero. He's the person for whom the joke stopped being funny at a very specific moment, for a very specific reason, and that's enough.

BEN'S PSYCHOLOGY

Ben finds out it was Eric. He already knows Eric ran it. His anger lands on Nick F and Jordan anyway — his pre-existing opps, the people his internal map of the group already marked as threats. Eric exists in a different category. Eric didn't redirect the blame. Ben's social geometry did it for him. This isn't confusion. This is Ben's relationship with the group in miniature: he knows where the power is, he knows where the danger is, and those are not the same place.

THE REAL SUBJECT

Surface: Eric catfished Ben with a fake Snapchat account for weeks while everyone watched.
Actually about: A group's unspoken, unanimous hierarchy — the unexamined agreement that one person's desire to be wanted is available for communal entertainment — and that person's conscious decision to accept the deal because the friendship on the other side of it is real.

THE QUESTION THE STORY ASKS

Can something be both cruel and genuine at the same time — and does it matter which one it is?

THE LAST LINE

"He came back. Honestly? We missed him. The Maria Brooke thing is still funny though."
