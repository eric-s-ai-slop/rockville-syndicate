Chapter: Maria Brooke

Eric spends weeks catfishing Ben through a fake Instagram account named Maria Brooke while the entire group watches and cheers in real time — and when Sean tells Ben both that it's a catfish and that Eric built it, Ben's anger lands on Nick F and Jordan anyway.

The group chat, primarily. Ben's DMs with Maria Brooke running parallel. One track practice, in person, where Sean is the one who closes the loop. 2022.

Present: Eric, Jordan, Nick F, Nick H, Maharko, Sean. Target: Ben.

## EMOTIONAL ARC

**Start:** The player feels like they're in on something good. The group is warm, the bit is landing, there's genuine pleasure in the shared joke. This is what being inside the circle feels like.

## CHAPTER IDENTITY

A nature documentary where the subject is also a member of the film crew, and doesn't know he's in the episode.

## MARIA BROOKE — WHO SHE IS (THE ARTIFACT)

Eric built her from the chatlog up. The account is an Instagram DM. She opens cold — "I'm doing a survey for Walter Johnson and I heard you're from there" — and withdraws it immediately in the next message: the survey ended yesterday. She's just here now. Ben says "you sure." She doesn't oversell. The pretext does its job and disappears.

She's casual, warm, slightly self-deprecating, never too eager. She deflects Snap gracefully ("no I stopped using it") with no explanation, no apology. Ben moves on without pushing. She mirrors Ben's world without being obvious: she names a real person — Ethan Rosner, a junior on the track team — as a mutual connection to make herself feel one degree away from real. She's getting into F1. She plays varsity volleyball and invites him to a game.

The moment the fiction crosses from entertainment to something else: Ben can't make her Saturday game because of a track meet at WJ. Maria Brooke responds: *"I didn't get to see you :("* Eric didn't build a fantasy. He built someone real enough to rearrange a person's schedule for.

**What the fiction extracted from Ben, literally:**
- A photo of his face. Sent to Eric. No caption. Just the image.
- A changed plan: Ben told Sean he was going to skip track practice to go watch her volleyball game.
- A real feeling, expressed to someone who doesn't exist.

**When it stopped being funny:** When Ben was about to skip a real obligation for it. Up until then it was a bit. After that it was something the group had done to someone.

## KEY DRAMATIC MOMENTS

1. **Eric sends the first message as Maria Brooke.** "I'm doing a survey for Walter Johnson and I heard you're from there." The account comes to life. He sends it and waits.

2. **The group chat assembles as an audience.** Everyone's in. Nobody hesitates. The bit is running.

3. **"u got snap?"** Ben tries to escalate. Maria says no, she stopped using it. The deflection is clean — no drama, no explanation. Ben moves on. The group's investment peaks: he's hooked and doesn't know the door is closed.

4. **The kart racing walk-back.** Ben mentions he's into racing — "only in rentals" but says he's getting pretty good. Maria asks "oh track?" Ben corrects himself immediately: "oh u meant track lmao." He'd been inflating slightly to impress her, got confused, and undersells himself in the same breath to correct the record. The group isn't just laughing at the slip. They're laughing because it confirms what they already knew: Ben is bitchless, and he just proved it to a girl he was trying to impress.

5. **Ben sends a photo of himself to Maria Brooke — to Eric.** No caption. Just his face. The group sees it. Whatever they say about it, they're saying it about Ben's face. The fiction has taken something real and made it available to everyone in the room.

6. **The Ethan Rosner gambit.** Maria name-drops Ethan Rosner — a junior on the track team, someone in Ben's actual orbit — as a mutual connection. Ben doesn't know him. She says he's a senior. The small-world construction is deliberate: Eric populated her with real nodes from Ben's social network so she'd feel adjacent-to-real, not invented.

7. **"I didn't get to see you :("** Ben tells Maria he has a track meet at WJ and can't make her volleyball game Saturday. She responds with that line. This is the moment the brief is about.

8. **Ben tells Sean he's going to skip track practice to watch her play volleyball.** The fiction touches something real. A real obligation, a real person who shares it.

9. **Sean tells Ben — both things.** At or around track practice, Sean tells Ben it's a catfish. And he names Eric. Sean doesn't have a moral awakening. He tells Ben because Ben was about to skip their shared real obligation for a person who doesn't exist — and that consequence lived in Sean's world, not just Ben's. He gives Ben everything: the fiction, and the architect.

10. **Ben blocks the account.** No confrontation. No message. Just a block. That's how the fiction ends on his end.

11. **Ben comes back. His anger lands on Nick F and Jordan.** Eric gave him the catfish. Sean gave him Eric's name. Ben's anger still lands on Nick F and Jordan — his pre-existing opps, the people his internal map of the group already marked as threats. Eric exists in a different category. The group is genuinely glad to have him back.

## THE PLAYER'S POSITION

The player is in the group chat from beat one. They can see Maria Brooke's messages and the group's reactions simultaneously. They are, from the first moment, complicit. They didn't build the account. They're watching, and laughing, and that's enough. They have information Ben doesn't. The question of what to do with it is theirs alone.

---

## THE CHOICE

Ben tells Sean he's going to skip track practice to watch Maria Brooke's volleyball game. The player has been in the group chat the whole time. They know everything.

**Say something now — before Sean does — or stay quiet and let it play out.**

Saying something costs: breaking the group's unspoken agreement, killing the bit, marking yourself as the one who looked away first. You were in the chat. You laughed. Saying something now means admitting that.

Staying quiet costs: you were there the whole time and said nothing. You're not different from anyone else. Maybe that's fine. Maybe that's exactly who you are in this group.

Neither option saves Ben. The choice is only about you.

---

## THE BOSS

Not Eric. The group chat — the collective, unanimous decision that kept the bit going. No one person made it. Everyone made it, including Sean, who was laughing right up until it cost something in his world too.

**The player is fighting their own complicity.**

Winning feels like: saying one true thing when it mattered, even knowing it wouldn't change the outcome.

Losing feels like: realizing you were laughing the whole time, and the laugh track was you.

## THE REAL SUBJECT

**Surface:** Eric catfished Ben with a fake Instagram account for weeks while everyone watched.

**Actually about:** A group's unspoken, unanimous hierarchy — the unexamined agreement that one person's desire to be wanted is available for communal entertainment — and that person's conscious decision to accept the deal because the friendship on the other side of it is real.

---

## THE QUESTION THE STORY ASKS

Can something be both cruel and genuine at the same time — and does it matter which one it is?

---

## HINGE CHARACTER PROFILES

**Sean**
Sean is peripheral — part of Ben's track world, getting closer to the Rockville group during this period. He was in the chat. He was laughing. He didn't have a moral awakening. He told Ben because Ben was about to skip their shared real obligation — track practice — for a person who didn't exist, and that consequence lived in Sean's world, not just Ben's. His intervention wasn't ethical. It was practical. He's not a hero. He's the person for whom the joke stopped being funny at a very specific moment, for a very specific reason, and that's enough.

What makes Sean the hinge: he doesn't just tell Ben it's a catfish. He tells Ben it was Eric. He closes both loops — the fiction and the architect — in one conversation. And it still doesn't redirect Ben's anger where you'd expect it to go.

**Ben**
Ben finds out it was Eric. He already knows Eric ran it — Sean told him directly. His anger still lands on Nick F and Jordan — his pre-existing opps, the people his internal map of the group already marked as threats. Eric exists in a different category. Eric didn't redirect the blame. Ben's social geometry did it for him. This isn't confusion. This is Ben's relationship with the group in miniature: he knows where the power is, he knows where the danger is, and those are not the same place. He comes back. He's fine. The bit is still funny to him too, eventually.
