# PROJECT OMEGA — CHAPTER BRIEF: THE UMBC INCIDENT

---

## LOGLINE

Ben washes out of LMU, comes home to a dead Rockville, gets invited to a UMBC frat party by Maharko, sexually assaults three girls in one night — and the group hears the story from the one person who was standing close enough to stop it and didn't.

---

## LOCATION AND WHO'S PRESENT

**Locations (in order):**
- The group chat (LMU fallout)
- Maharko's Audi S5 — Travis Scott playing
- UMBC frat basement — "Beauty and a Beat" by Justin Bieber ft. Nicki Minaj playing
- A parking lot at night (the confession)

**Present across the chapter:** Ben, Maharko, Eric, Nick F, Nick H. Multiple UMBC frat guys in one scene. Three girls are present in the basement but never named — they are treated as bodies in the space, and that is part of the point.

---

## EMOTIONAL ARC

**Start feeling:** Low-grade disappointment curdling into something sharper. Ben flunked out. The group saw it coming. The chat reacts with shrugs. The player is positioned slightly above Ben — the guy who couldn't keep it together.

**The turn:** The parking lot. Maharko describes "you're next" with the shhh motion. Nick F and Eric exchange a look. The disappointment hardens into recognition that Ben did something unforgivable — and that the person telling the story was close enough to have done something.

**End feeling:** Quiet, unresolved tension. Ben is gone. Maharko's story has a crack in it. The group doesn't pull on it. The player is left holding the question.

---

## CHAPTER IDENTITY

*A eulogy for someone who's still alive, delivered by a pallbearer who might have helped dig the grave.*

---

## KEY DRAMATIC MOMENTS

1. **The LMU verdict.** Group chat. Word spreads that Ben flunked out. Someone — Eric or Nick H — drops a line that's not cruel, just certain. The tone is set: Ben is the one who couldn't hold it.

2. **The invite.** Maharko texts Ben: "yo, want to go to umbc for a frat party? I got the stew." Nick H was the first choice; Ben is the fallback. Ben responds "hell yeah." The player sees eagerness that already reads as desperation.

3. **The drive.** Audi S5. Travis Scott on the speakers. Speeding, excited. The stew is in the car. Ben is already performing — louder than the moment calls for.

4. **The basement — the "you're next" frame.** Frat basement. "Beauty and a Beat" playing. Stew offered to girls: "hey, try this stew I brought." Ben drinks, moves through the room. Then the freeze-frame: Ben locks eyes with a girl, says "You're next," finger over mouth, shhh motion. In the background, visible but not in focus: Maharko. Watching.

5. **The escort.** Multiple frat guys. "Get your boy and leave." Ben is drunk, not fully processing. Maharko has to steer him out. Ben is told never to come back. The drive back: tense silence, or worse.

6. **The silence.** Ben goes quiet. Days pass. The group notices his absence. Eric and Nick F clock it. Something is wrong and nobody knows what yet.

7. **The parking lot.** Night. A parked car. Maharko tells Eric and Nick F what happened. He frames himself as a bystander who didn't see anything until the frat guys told him. When he describes "you're next" with the shhh motion, Nick F and Eric exchange a look. The math is silent: *he was close enough to hear that.* Nobody says it. The chapter's emotional verdict lands here.

8. **The distance begins.** The group pulls back from Ben. No confrontation, no ding-dong ditch — that comes in a later chapter. This one ends on the decision to let the space grow.

## THE PLAYER'S POSITION

The player is in the DM’s, then, in the Audi, then at the party. They hear Maharko's version. They see the look Nick F and Eric exchange. They know Ben did it. They also know Maharko's story has a hole. The chapter gives them no instructions on what to do with either fact.

---

## THE CHOICE

In the parking lot, after Maharko tells the story, the player decides how to respond to the crack they just heard.

**Option A: Push.** "You heard him say 'you're next.' How close were you standing?"  
Cost: Maharko gets defensive. The group's clean verdict on Ben frays. The player risks friction with someone who's staying in the group.

**Option B: Let it go.** "He actually said that? What the hell."  
Cost: Ben is exiled cleanly, the group holds together, and the player knows they chose not to look.

## THE BOSS — BEN: THE GHOST AT THE PARTY

**Intro lines:**
"BEN — The Ghost at the Party"
"Cut through the version of the night he told himself."

**Arena:** The UMBC frat basement, stylized as a hazy memory-space. "Beauty and a Beat" loops, distorted. The stew sits on a table, glowing. Three shadowy figures representing the girls drift at the edges of the screen. Maharko's silhouette flickers in the corner — sometimes present, sometimes gone, never attackable.

**The fight:** Ben isn't fought physically. The player battles his *self-justifications* — defensive lines that manifest as attacks. Each phase is a rationalization the player breaks through by selecting the right counter from dialogue options.

- **Phase 1 — "I was just having fun."** Counters: evidence of the first girl's reaction, the frat guys' testimony, Maharko's own detail.
- **Phase 2 — "She was into it."** Counters: the "you're next" line, the shhh motion, the pattern across three girls.
- **Phase 3 — "Maharko was there too."** A deflection. The player can choose to pull that thread or stay on Ben. Pulling it adds a permanent note to Maharko's profile but doesn't change Ben's culpability.
- **Final phase — Silence.** Ben stops fighting. Just stands there. The player lands one last hit: "You knew what you were doing." The fight ends.

**Winning feels like:** The truth snaps into focus. Ben did it. Maharko saw. The group made their choice. The player sees all the pieces.

**Losing feels like:** The justifications hold. The story stays blurry. The player becomes one of the people who didn't push.

## THE REAL SUBJECT

**Surface conflict:** Ben's sexual assault at UMBC and the group's response.

**What it's actually about:** A guy who already lost one life, got one night to feel like he mattered, and burned his last bridge — while the person who brought him there told a story that kept himself out of the ashes.

---

## THE QUESTION THE STORY ASKS

Can you do something that makes you unworthy of the people who knew you before — and does it matter if nobody ever asks for your side?

---

## HINGE CHARACTER PROFILE — MAHARKO

Maharko is the Wildcard, but in this chapter he's something more precise: the only witness who keeps talking. He invites Ben as a fallback after Nick H can't go. He brings the stew. He's in the basement, close enough to hear Ben say "you're next" and do the shhh motion, close enough that the frat guys find him to deliver the ultimatum. When he tells the group, he frames himself as a bystander who didn't see anything until it was too late. The timeline doesn't hold — if the frat guys knew by the third incident, they would have pulled Ben off before Maharko could "witness" it. The group notices the hole and doesn't push. Maharko stays in the group, his reputation quietly dented. His relationship with Ben is over. The player never learns the full truth. The chapter doesn't answer whether he was complicit, cowardly, or actively framing Ben — it just shows the crack and lets the player decide how much it bothers them.

---

## HINGE CHARACTER PROFILE — NICK F & ERIC (AS A UNIT)

The Moderator and the Admin functioning as dual detectives. They notice Ben's silence. They corner Maharko in the parking lot. They hear "you're next" and exchange the look. They understand, together, that Maharko was closer than he's saying. And then they let it go. They focus on Ben. They lead the distance. The chapter doesn't judge them. It just notes: when it was easier to lose Ben than to question Maharko, they chose the easier thing.

---

## ARTIFACT — THE STEW

A drink mixture Ben and Maharko bring to parties. Its function is an opener to approach girls: "hey, try this stew I brought." It's infamous, permanently linked to them as a duo. In this chapter, it's the prop Ben uses to start what ends him. After UMBC, the stew is retired. It doesn't appear in later chapters. Its last service was this basement.

---

## CONNECTION TO OPERATION DING DONG DITCH BEN

This chapter ends with the distance established and the verdict formed. The ding-dong ditch — the group screaming "we know what you did" through Ben's door — is a separate chapter already documented. This chapter feeds directly into it: the parking lot is where the sentence is written. The doorbell is where it's delivered.
