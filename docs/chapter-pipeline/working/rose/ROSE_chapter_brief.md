# CREATIVE BRIEF — "ROSE"

July 4th, 2024. Nick F is visiting Maharko in Florida. At a July 4th party, Maharko feeds a 16-year-old named Rose alcohol until she's half-conscious, gets caught pulling her up off the lawn, and makes out with her in the back of a moving car while Alex tells him to stop, Nick F photographs it, and Jordan says nothing.

- **The lawn** — outside the party, end of the night. Rose on the ground, Maharko pulling her up. Nick F photographing.

## Chapter identity

A real-time chapter set in a moving car in Boca Raton on July 4th, 2024, where the player discovers that the Boca Syndicate is a closed system — every move costs something, no move prevents the thing from happening, and the only question is which loss the player chooses to carry. The comedy engine runs until it can't. Then the car keeps moving anyway. Within the Boca Syndicate, you can't win.

The player sees what the group sees. The player sees Rose the way the group sees Rose — present but opaque, seen but not known. ## The choice

**The car, after the makeout.** The player has to decide what to do, right now, in the back seat of a moving car, in a city they don't live in, surrounded by people who aren't from their home syndicate.

- **Option A — Push hard. Confront Maharko directly.**
  **Cost:** Maharko turns on you. You're a Rockville kid in a Boca car. You have no ride home that isn't this car. You have no friends in this city beyond the people in this car. The Boca Syndicate closes ranks — you're the outsider who came to a party and started a fight. Alex already backed down; you're alone. The car keeps moving. The makeout already happened. You said the thing out loud. The thing is still done. You're estranged from the Florida crew, alone in their city, and Rose is still in the back seat. You leave Boca with an enemy and without having prevented anything.

- **Option B — Stay silent. Let the car keep moving.**
  **Cost:** The car keeps moving. The makeout happens. You watch. You become one of them — not legally, not loudly, but in the way that matters: you saw it, you said nothing, and the car arrived home. You don't get to leave this night behind. The photos get taken. The photos get shared. You're in the chat when they get shared. You're in the group when Maharko stays in the group. You joined the Boca Syndicate tonight. You don't get to unjoin. *(Canon. The group chose B. The group is still choosing B.)*

- **Option C — Turn on Nick F. Demand he delete the photos.**
  **Cost:** Nick F is your Rockville connection. You'll see him every day back home. Burning him means burning your home base. And — demanding he delete the photos means destroying the only evidence that this happened. Maharko gets to deny it. The record vanishes. You've protected Rose's image (maybe) and destroyed the proof. The Boca Syndicate keeps Maharko. The Rockville Syndicate loses its evidence. You chose the document over the documenter and lost both.

Each option costs something the player can't get back. Option A costs the player's standing in Boca and doesn't prevent the act. Option B costs the player's conscience and locks them into the syndicate. Option C costs the player's Rockville base and destroys the evidence. **There is no Option D.** The chapter does not offer a clean exit. The narrator has already said so, six lines ago. Within the Boca Syndicate, you can't win.

Option B is canon because it's the option that requires the least courage and costs the least *tonight* — and the group has, demonstrably, for two years, chosen the option that costs the least tonight. The cost compounds later. The chapter doesn't show later. The chapter just shows the choice, and the player carries it.

## The boss

**MAHARKO — The Florida Wildcard / Defeat him before the car gets home.**

Triggered by Option A (and Option C, with modified defense order). If the player chose Option B, the boss fight is skipped — the chapter goes straight to the drive home, and the last line lands without the accounting ever being said.

For players who chose A or C, the boss fight is a four-phase confrontation in the back seat of the moving car. Each phase is a defense Maharko uses, in real time, to justify what he just did:

- **Phase 1: "I needed this."** The need defense. Maharko frames it as inevitability — he was desperate, he hadn't had success with girls in Florida. The player breaks it by naming that need does not create right.
- **Phase 2: "She liked me."** The mind-reading defense. Maharko claims he could tell she was into him. The player breaks it by naming that a half-conscious 16-year-old cannot like you in a way that means anything.
- **Phase 3: "She's into it. Look at her."** The evidence defense. Maharko points at Rose's state as evidence of consent. The player breaks it by naming what incapacitation actually means.
- **Phase 4: "Stop cockblocking me."** The threat. The final defense. Maharko threatens the player the way he threatened Alex. The player breaks it by not backing down. The car keeps moving. The boss is defeated. Maharko is still Maharko. The makeout still happened. The accounting is on the record — but the record is said, not acted on.

**Winning feels like:** the accounting is said. The four defenses have been named and broken, out loud, in the back seat. But the car keeps moving. The makeout still happened. You are now estranged from the Boca Syndicate, alone in Florida, with no ride home and no friends in this city. You said the thing. The thing is still done. You won the fight. You lost the night. Rose is still in the back seat.

**Losing feels like:** the accounting is never said. The defenses stand. The car keeps moving. You pushed and failed. The Boca Syndicate closes ranks around Maharko. You're an outsider who started a fight and lost it. Rose is still in the back seat. The photos still exist. You go home to Rockville knowing what happened and knowing no one in Boca will ever speak to you about it again.

**Either way:** Rose is in the back seat. The car keeps moving. The photos are on Nick F's phone. You can't win. You can only choose which loss you carry home.

## The last line

**"Rose is 16. She is in the back seat of a car going home. She does not know about the photos."**

Three sentences. Present tense. Short. The narrator, who has been gossipy and taking sides all chapter, goes quiet for one line and just looks at Rose. The "she does not know about the photos" points forward — to the chat, to the two years, to everything downstream — without leaving the night. It's a present-tense fact about what Rose doesn't know, at the end of this night. The photos exist (Nick F took them, during the chapter). Rose doesn't know. That's the gap the rest of her life is going to be. The chapter doesn't follow her into it. The chapter ends here, in the car, with Rose not knowing.

(If the player chose Option B — silence — the last line is the same. Rose doesn't know either way. The difference is whether the player said anything out loud first. Rose still doesn't know.)

## The real subject

**Surface conflict:** Maharko fed a 16-year-old alcohol and made out with her while she was half-conscious, in the back of a moving car, and the people around him failed to stop it.

**What it's actually about:** A closed system. The Boca Syndicate is a gravity well — Maharko is the sun, Jordan orbits without friction, Alex tried to break orbit and got pulled back, Nick F documents instead of deflecting. The player enters this system as a guest (Rockville visitor in Boca territory) and discovers that guests don't have leverage. The car is the closed system made literal: four people, moving, can't open the door, can't stop the night, can't win. The chapter is the night the player learns that the Syndicate is not a friend group you can reform from inside. It's a thing you either join or leave. And leaving costs everything. Joining costs everything else.
