*Eric's room, present day. Where you came in.*

*Eric is not playing. There is exactly one piece left, and it's the one he loaded

ERIC *(aloud, to no one — evenly; the toast, finally given)*: "So. The summer Maharko

ERIC: "—I did it. The calls were me. Both phones, every time, all eight months. Jacob
never called anyone. Nobody ever called anyone. There was a website, and there was me,
and I thought he'd be good content. / And he was. / That's the whole sentence. It was
never long. It was just heavy."

*He doesn't ask for anything. Nothing on screen grants anything. That's not the scene.
It was never going to be that scene.*

*A breath.*

ERIC *(to the screen — meaning: to all of you)*: "You were made. / Does it matter?"

*He does not answer it.*

*Nothing answers it.*

*Everything stops.*

*Whatever was still running anywhere — under the menus, under the room tone — stops
with it. The light steps down until the room is shapes.*

*You can still move. That's not an error. Walk it if you want: the desk, the bed, the
door. The door doesn't go anywhere. Nothing prompts. Nothing glows. Nothing spawns.
Twelve chapters of this game have trained you to wait for the next thing, and the next
thing does not come.*

*Stay as long as you want. It won't develop.*

*And somewhere in the staying, notice that the dark has stopped being specifically his.
A dark room with one person in it and a question on the floor isn't a set anymore. He
asked all of you at once. It's all of your rooms now.*

*Hold.*

*Hold past comfortable. Past the length that means a dramatic pause. Out to the length
that means the chapter is over — because it is. This is where it ends. This, right
here, is the last thing it has to say, and it is saying it.*

*Then, on the desk: one buzz.*

*Short. Real. The sound your own phone makes.*

*The phone — face-down since the first minute — lights the desk around its own edges.*

*He turns it over.*

**Sub Zero Squad**

**JACOB: who tryna go to mcdonalds tn?**

*That's all it says. It's all it ever says.*

*Why him. Why tonight. The chapter doesn't know. It told you a while ago that it was

across the finish line: no reason on record, no meaning assigned. Here is the entire

*This is yours. Last one.*

**ERIC: omw**

*He's gone.*

*Fade — no music, no jingle, nothing this game has ever used to tell you that you won,
because you didn't — on the empty room, the empty chair, and the phone on the desk,
face-up, still lit.*

*The light it makes is small.*

*It's on, though.*

**SUBTEXT NOTES — Scene 11**
- **THE NARRATOR RETIRES AT THE QUESTION — the coda's structural rule.** NARRATOR
  (ERIC) never speaks again after "Does it matter?"; everything that follows is
  italic staging and two texts. The chapter opened with the narrator not yet talking
  (Scene 0) and closes with him done talking — the bookend is formal and must not be
  broken by any late narration, caption, or epilogue card. Spoken-Eric delivers the
  confession and the question aloud, in the Scene 0 stage grammar ("to the screen —
  meaning: to all of you"), completing that scene's device.
- **The completed sentence is the payoff of Scene 0's aborted one and must keep its
  seams:** same opening words ("So. The summer Maharko left for Florida. What happened
  was—"), the dash landing exactly where "simply" used to live, and the staging beat
  about no word arriving to make it smaller. He finally says the plain thing; he does
  not apologize (locked since Batch 3: the confession is the artifact; an apology is a
  request and nothing is requested).
- **THE LONG HOLD (beat 3) is the chapter's longest protected silence — uncuttable and
  deliberately over-long.** `stopAllAudio`, tint toward dark, room interactive with
  nowhere to go, no prompt of any kind. It must run long enough to read as the actual
  ending; the buzz must feel like it arrives after the chapter is over. Phase 7's
  compression contract should mark every wait in beats 3–5 as protected.
- **THE SINGLE BUZZ is a named protected sound — one sfx, once.** No ringtone, no
  swell, and no `victory_jingle` anywhere, ever (standing chapter rule). The
  face-down→face-up phone is the coda's entire prop language: face-down in Scene 0,
  face-down through the hold, turned over for Sub Zero Squad, left face-up at the
  fade. The edge-glow echo of Scene 3's face-down phone stays uncaptioned.
- **Jacob's opacity holds through the last frame:** sender name and one line, both
  grounded (the "who tryna go to mcdonalds tn?" ritual is verbatim-recurring in the
  archive; Jacob-as-instigator is the inversion record). The staging says plainly that
  the chapter doesn't know why he texts and assigns no meaning — that refusal is the
  final firing of the opacity rule and may not be softened into warmth ("he forgives,"
  "he knows") or irony. He shows up. That is all the record supports, and it is
  enough.
- **"omw" is typed by the player (M6)** — three letters, lowercase, staged with the
  same "Type it" grammar as Scene 6's "why." The rhyme (the player's first typed word
  covered the crime; their last one answers the summons) is real, intentional, and
  NEVER captioned — "Three letters, lowercase." is the entire permitted surface. No
  dialogue, text, or reply lands after "omw."
- The pilot light must stay small enough that the silence wins: the final couplet
  ("The light it makes is small. / It's on, though.") is the coda's one degree above
  cold and its ceiling. Any pass that adds warmth after it — a second text, a heart, a
  chime, group laughter over the fade — breaks the ending spec. Fade on the empty room
  with the phone light on; keys leave with Eric (the man who now drives himself to a
  McDonald's at night stays staging-only, uncaptioned).
