## SCENE 2 — "The Trophy"

weeks after the McDonald's night. Maharko is in Florida.
**Present:** Nick F (driver's seat), Nick H (passenger), Eric (back seat).
**Emotional temp:** 4 — a comedy scene with a listener in it.
**One-line job:** The Nicks tell the McDonald's story as a trophy and hand Eric the
material without knowing they are handing anyone anything; the joke indicts its tellers
a second time; and for the first time in the past, the camera is with Eric — and holds
him one beat too long.

---

sunsets like a man doing a job. / One in the morning, a Thursday — technically a

closed. / Why are they here? For no reason. That's important. There was no reason."

finished — in the back seat.*

NICK F: "THE STATS CLASS. Bro, Maharko goes — Maharko looks at him dead in the eye and

NICK H *(flat, the second stroke of the couplet)*: "Closing arguments. At the

NICK H: "Around, twice."

NICK H: "And that's the funniest part, though. Like — think about it. WHO is at a

NICK F: "On a Tuesday."

*Hold on the back seat.*

*Hold one beat longer than the scene has a reason for.*

## SCENE 3 — "The Bit That Runs Itself"

**Location:** L2 — the void: two lit bedroom-islands in black. Left: Nick F's room.
Right: Jacob's room. Between them, nothing. Group Chat overlay throughout.
**Present:** Nick F (left island); Jacob (right island — EXTERIOR ONLY, hard rule);
Nick H and Eric in the Group Chat overlay. Summer 2024 → October.
**Emotional temp:** 5 — the funniest stretch of the chapter, increasingly itchy.
**One-line job:** The double-call ritual as the group lived it — the founding text
lands as comedy, then the voicemail phantom gets Jacob credited for genius he never
performed; both phones visibly ring the whole time and the player reads straight past
it.

---

NARRATOR (THE GROUP CHAT): "A few days later. / Two bedrooms, eleven-forty on a
Wednesday night. / On the left: Nick F's room. On the right: Jacob's room. / Between
them: nothing. Rockville, presumably."

*The left room you could reconstruct from sound alone: a gaming chair that cost more
than the desk it sits at, a monitor doing most of the lighting, a hoodie on the floor
still holding the shape of its owner, a fitted sheet in a long-term dispute with its
mattress.*

*The right room is made. That's the word for it. The bed is made — at eleven-forty at
night, the bed is made. On the desk there is a stats textbook, open, a highlighter
lying in the gutter of the spine. Car keys on a hook by the door, because the keys have
a hook.*

*Two rooms, two lamps, and a long black nothing between them.*

*Then — at the same moment — a phone lights up in each one, and both of them start
ringing.*

*On the left, Nick F's screen says JACOB. He grins at the ceiling before he answers.
This is already a bit and it hasn't even started.*

NICK F: "Jacob?"

JACOB: "Hey. Hi. What's up?"

NICK F: "What's up with YOU, man. You called me."

*Across the void, Jacob doesn't move for a second.*

JACOB: "No — you called me. My phone rang, and it was your name, so I picked it up.
That's the whole thing that happened on my end."

*Beat.*

*It is not a mean question, the way Nick F says it. That is the thing about Nick F —

Jacob.*

JACOB: "I don't know why. That's why I answered by saying 'what's up.'"

NICK F: "I—"

**NICK F: he did it again**

**SUBTEXT NOTES — Scene 3**
- **THE PLANTED EVIDENCE — handle like ordnance.** Both phones ring, incoming, at the
  same instant, every recurrence — it is on screen for the entire scene and it is NEVER
  captioned, zoomed, or remarked. The staging protects it three ways: (1) only Nick F's
  screen ever shows a caller ID; Jacob's phone just rings, and a ringing phone on "the
  caller's" side parses as calling; (2) the simultaneity is stated once, plainly, in
  the first recurrence, then never repeated in words — later recurrences just show it;
  (3) every ring is immediately buried under the next funny beat, because speed is the
  misdirection. If any pass adds a shot of Jacob's screen, a narrator aside, or a
  musical sting on the double-ring, the chapter's best replay payload dies here.
- **Jacob opacity, hard rule:** he is exterior only — room, posture, complete sentences,
  a made bed, a highlighter, keys on a hook. The room testifies to a careful person; no
  line of narration may testify to his feelings. His dialogue stays plain, evidenced,
  fully articulate, and received as a bit. Never write him flustered; write him not
  received.
- The voicemail greeting must be rendered exactly once, in full, politeness intact
  ("Thanks."). The comedy is Nick F's awe; the wound is that the "script" is a polite
  man's actual voicemail playing to a line he never dialed. Do not let the greeting be
  funny in itself. The three Oct-10 texts are verbatim canon and load in exact order —
  the group's one act of respect, spent on dead air.
- "Between them: nothing. Rockville, presumably." is this scene's single hidden plant
  (the nothing is where the third island is). One per scene; do not add another; do not
  explain it. The scene's final line ("it finds a third") refers to a phone, not the
  island — keep that ambiguity exactly as is.
- Eric in the overlay: ONE text in recurrence one ("what did he want" — the question he
  knows the answer to), then silence for the rest of the scene. His sparseness is the
  profile ("winning = one lowercase word and out"); on replay it is the confession. No
  staging may point at it.
- Nick H's overlay lines outside the canon pair stay teasing-sideways ("he just wanted
  to hear your voice" — aimed at Nick F, indicting the group's read of Jacob without
  minting new cruelty). The ethics gate holds: no invented insult may exceed the
  archive's own register, and none may land a new blow on Jacob.

## SCENE 4 — "The Wrong Man Confesses For You"

**Location:** L2 — the same two-island void. October, still.
**Present:** Nick F (left island); Jacob (right island — exterior only); BEN, by phone,
voice only; Nick H absent; Eric present ONLY as the Group Chat overlay.
**Emotional temp:** 6 — the laugh still works; the floor under it doesn't.
**One-line job:** The blast radius — Ben calls to say the group is behind the calls,

---

NARRATOR (THE GROUP CHAT): "October, still. A Sunday, late. / The two rooms again. The

JACOB."

*The screen says BEN.*

NARRATOR: "You know Ben. / Or — the group used to know Ben. He used to be around. Then

NICK F: "…Ben?"

BEN: "Is something going on with Jacob?"

*He's already smiling. The drawer has something in it after all.*

BEN: "He keeps calling me, man. A lot. Fourteen times since Tuesday — I counted,

BEN: "That's my whole point. He wouldn't. I barely know the guy."

*A beat.*

BEN: "Somebody's making this happen. And I think it's you guys."

*He doesn't say it like an accusation. He says it the way you name a song from two

*Nick F covers the mic. To his empty room, with total sincerity:*

NICK F: "This is insane."

*It is the voice of a man being handed content. Somewhere under it, unreachable, is

BEN: "It's not funny. Whatever this is. Just—"

*A breath on the line.*

BEN: "—whoever's doing it. Tell them I asked them to stop. You don't have to believe

NICK F: "There's no 'them,' bro."

*Quiet on the line. It goes on longer than any pause Nick F has ever let stand in his

BEN: "Yeah. Okay. Good night, Nick."

*Click. Ben hangs up first, politely, the second person in this story to exit saying

*Nick F looks at the dead phone for a second. Then the thumbs start.*

**NICK F: Bro Ben just called me**

*The reply comes fast.*

**ERIC: why**

**ERIC: 💀**

**NICK F: Apperantly Jacob is spamming him and he thinks were causing it**

**NICK F: Bro Jacob is a LOSER**

  catching up on the thread. Second read: the operator confirming his blast radius in

- The planted evidence peaks here and stays uncaptioned: Ben describes the ringing the
