## SCENE 0 — "The Last Save Slot"

**Location:** L0 — Eric's room, present day. Deep night.
**Present:** Eric, alone.
**Emotional temp:** 2 — but wrong. Quiet in a way no chapter has opened.
**One-line job:** Signal in sixty seconds that this chapter is not an adventure — it is
addressed to you; establish the confession frame: he cannot say it, so he built it.

---

*No music.*

*Eleven chapters of this game have opened the same way: a needle drop, and the
narrator's voice arriving like a friend sliding into the booth across from you. This one
opens with room tone — the specific silence of a house where everyone else is asleep and
one light is still on.*

*Eric's room, present day. The overhead light is off. The desk lamp is off. The light is
all monitor, and the monitor is showing the game. This game. The chapter select screen:
eleven tiles, eleven cover images the whole group can name on sight, because the group
was there for every one of them.*

*Below the eleventh tile, a twelfth slot. No image. No title. The cursor is sitting
on it.*

*Eric is not playing. He has his hand on the mouse and he is not moving it, the way you
sit at a green light when you're not sure the turn is legal.*

He has shipped eleven chapters about these people. He has made content out of everyone's
worst night, including several of his own, and the group has laughed at all of it,
together, in the room, which is the whole point of the game and — he would say, if
asked — the whole point of him. This is the only story he never pitched.

He has done the math on "two years" more times than he'd admit, because the math keeps
coming back unflattering. Two years is long enough for a secret to outlive its reasons.
Early on he had reasons — good ones, whole rosters of them, people the truth would hurt
worse than the lie. He went through that roster recently, name by name, checking who the
silence is still protecting.

Every name came back the same. Just you.

*He picks up his phone. Opens the group chat. The keyboard rises to meet his thumb the
way it has ten thousand times.*

*He types:* **I need to tell you guys something.**

*Seven words. True, correctly spelled, impossible. He looks at them long enough for the
screen to dim; taps it awake; looks some more.*

*He deletes the message one character at a time. There is a way to delete it all at
once. He does it one character at a time.*

ERIC *(aloud, to no one — evenly, like a man rehearsing a toast)*: "So. The summer
Maharko left for Florida. What happened was, I simply—"

*He stops.*

There it is. *Simply.* The word arrives on schedule, the way it has arrived at the front
of every sentence he has ever needed to survive, and for the first time in his life he
can hear it from the outside: a word you put in front of a thing to make the thing
smaller.

There is no version of this sentence where the thing is small.

*He does not finish the sentence. He has never finished the sentence. He has gotten this
far in a parked car with Nick H, at a kitchen counter with Nick F, and once standing
directly behind Jacob with the whole thing loaded. The sentence does not have a second
half when he is the one holding it. That is why the twelfth slot exists.*

He has always been better at building things than at saying them. He is aware that this
is not a virtue. It is, in fact, most of the confession.

*He puts the phone face-down on the desk. He clicks the twelfth slot. A title field
opens, cursor blinking, asking for the one thing he is usually best at. He has named
everything this group has ever done — every era, every bit, half the people in it. Every
name he can think of for this one is a joke, and every joke is a door out of the room.*

*He leaves the title empty.*

ERIC *(to the screen — meaning: to all of you)*: "You're going to laugh at the first
part."

*A breath.*

ERIC: "You should. It was funny. That was never the problem."

*He presses enter. The screen light changes on his face.*

*And for the first time in twelve chapters, somebody else starts talking.*

---

**SUBTEXT NOTES — Scene 0**
- The wrongness is formal, not stated: **no music and no narrator.** The narrator's
  absence IS the cold open — it pays off at the Scene 5 snap (the narrator was Eric all
  along; in Scene 0 he simply hasn't started telling yet, and "somebody else starts
  talking" turns bitter on replay). Protect both silences in beat-translation; the pause
  after "I simply—" is uncuttable.
- He never says what the thing is. The scene teases the era ("the summer Maharko left
  for Florida") and nothing else. The confession frame is carried by the typed-and-
  deleted text and the unfinished sentence — not by content.
- "It was funny. That was never the problem." licenses the Act I comedy and pre-indicts
  it in the same breath — the thesis at whisper volume. It must stay the last spoken
  line before the narrator arrives.

## SCENE 1 — "Two Ladders, One McDonald's"

**Location:** L1 — McDonald's interior, Rockville Pike, ~3 a.m., summer 2024 — rendered
as a REENACTMENT: the night as the Nicks tell it, performed over the scene itself.
**Present:** Nick F, Nick H, Maharko (booth); Jacob and Chris Rivas (arriving); one
employee. Narration: The Group Chat, full register, with v.o. intrusions from Nick F and
Nick H (the telling). Eric: not present, not mentioned. His hand stays hidden.
**Emotional temp:** 3 — laughter with a wire in it.
**One-line job:** The founding cruelty, funny the way it was funny at the time AND
legible as cruelty — the Nicks audition upward for Maharko by stepping on Jacob; Jacob
steps on Chris one rung down; and only the player notices who else is at a McDonald's at
3 a.m.

---

NARRATOR (THE GROUP CHAT): "Summer 2024. Rockville Pike, three in the morning. / The
McDonald's on the Pike never fully closes and never fully opens. It exists around the
clock in a third state — lights on, floor wet, one employee on headset who has chosen,
wisely, to believe the lobby is empty. / Tonight, the lobby is not empty."

*The booth by the window: Nick F, Nick H, Maharko. Three people in a four-person booth.
The trays were cleared a while ago; what remains is cups, straw wrappers folded into
tiny accordions, and no reason. They have been here forty minutes. Nobody remembers
deciding to come.*

NARRATOR: "Why are they here? The official record says nothing. The parking lot, if you
could depose it, would say slightly more."

NICK F (V.O. — the telling): "Okay, so it's like two in the morning—"

NICK H (V.O.): "Three."

NICK F (V.O.): "—it's three in the morning, and we're just posted at the McDonald's,
right? For no reason. That's important. There was no reason."

*Above the soda fountain, the wall clock spins obligingly from two to three. The scene
does what the telling tells it. Get used to that.*

NARRATOR: "You're getting this night the way everyone got this night: secondhand. The
Nicks tell it well. They've had practice."

*In the booth, Maharko has the window seat, which is not a seat so much as a throne that
came with the booth. He leaves for Florida in nine days. Nobody has said this out loud
tonight, but it is in the room the way a deadline is in a room, and the Nicks have been
performing at him for forty straight minutes the way you perform at someone who is about
to stop being able to see you.*

MAHARKO *(apropos of nothing, with total authority)*: "This location has the best Sprite
in the county. That's a fact. I'm not discussing it."

NICK H: "It's the carbonation."

MAHARKO: "It's the carbonation."

*Nick H's eyes flick — very briefly — to Maharko's face, checking whether the agreement
scored. This is the thing to watch tonight. Every line the Nicks say has a follow-up
glance attached, submitted upward, and Maharko's face is the scoreboard: a nose-exhale
is a point; nothing is nothing.*

NICK F: "Honestly the ice hits different here too, I feel like the Pike locations just
care more, like as a region—"

*Nothing. Nick F drums the table and rallies.*

NARRATOR: "Then: headlights."

*Across the window, headlights sweep the booth — an older Camry, parking carefully,
between the lines, at three in the morning, an hour at which the lines have stopped
mattering to everyone else on earth.*

NICK F (V.O.): "And then — bro. The door."

*The door chimes. Two silhouettes. First: Jacob, keys still in hand. Behind him: a kid
in a hoodie who catches the door on its backswing and closes it gently, like the
building is asleep.*

NARRATOR: "The one with the keys is Jacob. You know Jacob. / The other one — hold on.
His name is Chris Rivas, and he is the only person who will enter this McDonald's
tonight because he wanted food."

*Nick F sees them and transforms. He rises out of the booth — actually rises — arms
opening, face igniting, a maître d' at the restaurant of this exact moment.*

NICK F: "JACOB? No. No way. NO way."

NARRATOR: "The warmth is real. Keep that somewhere safe. It's real, and it will not
help."

*Jacob comes over. Of course he comes over. He crosses the lobby and lands at the end of
the table — at the exact spot where standing becomes hovering.*

*There is room in the booth. There has been room in the booth for about a year.*

*Nobody moves over.*

NICK F: "What are you doing here, man?! It's three in the morning!"

JACOB: "Chris was hungry. I was up anyway, so I drove us."

*Beat.*

*Nick H laughs into his cup. Nick F laughs because Nick H is laughing. Maharko produces
a single nose-exhale — and both Nicks clock it like judges' scores going up.*

NICK H *(to the table, about Jacob, in front of Jacob)*: "At least he drove himself."

NICK F: "No, that's honestly so responsible—"

NICK H *(to Jacob now — the second half of the couplet)*: "But like — who's at a
McDonald's at three in the morning, man?"

*The booth detonates. Jacob laughs too.*

*Nobody follows the question anywhere. It's 3:04. The scene moves on.*

*At the counter, Chris orders. He orders like a person who knew his order in the car. He
says thank you to the cashier twice — once at the start, once at the end. He does not
come over to the booth. He posts up at the pickup counter and checks his phone.*

NICK F *(sotto, leaning in, delighted)*: "Who's the friend?"

JACOB *(a half-turn toward the counter; his voice drops a floor)*: "That's Chris. We're
not really — he lives near me. He was hungry, so."

*The same move, one rung down.*

*Nobody at the table notices, because from inside a ladder all anyone ever looks is up.*

MAHARKO *(flat; not unkind, which is worse)*: "You're the same as junior year, bro."

*The Nicks detonate again — bigger this time, because it's Maharko, and tribute
compounds.*

JACOB: "I don't think that's true. I have a job this summer. I'm taking a stats class.
People change. I don't think I'm the same."

*It is a complete answer. It has evidence in it. He is standing at the end of a table at
three in the morning, holding his keys, defending the proposition that he is a person
who changes.*

*The booth receives the whole thing as a bit. Nick F is wheezing. Nick H says "the stats
class—" like a man quoting a punchline that hasn't finished being said. Maharko exhales
through his nose twice, which is a standing ovation.*

NARRATOR: "For the record: it wasn't a bit. / For the other record — the one this group
actually keeps — it was."

CHRIS *(from the counter; the only thing he says all scene)*: "Jacob. Food."

*Jacob stands halfway between the counter and the booth for exactly one second — the
geometry of the rest of his year, if anyone were charting it. (Nobody is charting it
yet.)*

*He goes to the counter. Takes the bag. And then — this is the part the telling always
includes, complete with impression — he comes back. He comes all the way back to the
booth to say:*

JACOB: "We're around all summer. If you guys are ever doing something — I'm around."

*Around, twice.*

NICK F *(instantly, warmly, with his whole chest)*: "Yes. Dude. For sure. For sure, for
sure."

NARRATOR: "Nick F means it the way you mean things at three in the morning: completely,
and not at all."

*Jacob nods. He taps the table twice — a little goodbye drumroll — and goes. Chris is
already holding the door.*

*Through the window: the Camry's lights come on. It reverses carefully. It signals —
signals, at three in the morning, to an audience of nobody — and pulls onto the Pike.*

NARRATOR: "That is the last time the group ever sees Chris Rivas. He got his food. He
said thank you twice. As exits from this story go, his is the clean one."

*One second of silence. Two.*

*Then the booth goes up like a stadium.*

NICK F: "BRO."

NICK H: "The stats class."

NICK F: "'I'm around all summer' — bro, he came BACK to say it. He had the food. He was
OUT. He came back—"

*And here is the thing about the next ten minutes: Nick F performs the last ten minutes
for the two people who were there for it. That is how you know a story has a future —
it gets its first telling before the door finishes closing.*

MAHARKO *(the verdict, flat as the table)*: "He'd come back tomorrow if you texted him."

*Both Nicks laugh. Neither of them hears it as the truest thing said all night —
possibly because it is also the meanest, and the booth has never once needed to tell
those two apart.*

NICK F (V.O.): "And that's the funniest part though. Like — think about it. WHO is at a
McDonald's at three in the morning?"

NICK H (V.O.): "On a Tuesday."

*Hold on the booth: three of them, at the McDonald's, at 3:11 in the morning, on a
Tuesday, laughing.*

*Hold one second longer than is comfortable.*

NARRATOR: "The Nicks will tell this story for weeks, to anybody who missed it. /
Eventually they'll tell it to exactly the right person. / That's next."

**SUBTEXT NOTES — Scene 1**
- The core irony — the tellers were ALSO at McDonald's at 3 a.m. — is placed twice
  (Nick H live, "who's at a McDonald's at three in the morning, man?"; Nick F in the
  v.o. reprise) and is NEVER captioned. No narrator wink, no character noticing. The
  only underline permitted is the held final shot of the booth at 3:11. If a later pass
  adds a lampshade, the scene dies.
- Chris Rivas is protected by four beats that must all survive compression: the
  "only person who came here for food" intro, the two thank-yous, his one line
  ("Jacob. Food."), and the factual exit epitaph. Cut any one and he becomes a
  punchline by omission — the exact crime the chapter is confessing.
- Silences and blocking that carry the meaning: nobody ever offers Jacob the seat (the
  empty fourth seat is Ben's vacancy, unnamed); Jacob's lines stay complete and
  evidenced and are received as a bit (plants the transcoding motif Scene 7 pays off —
  see the two-ledgers narrator couplet); "(Nobody is charting it yet.)" is the scene's
  single hidden Eric-plant — do not add more, and do not explain it.
