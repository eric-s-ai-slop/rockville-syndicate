# Ben’s Life

Ben was always the punching bag of the group.

Everyone made fun of him constantly, whether it was because of what he wore, what he said, or whatever ridiculous plan he had committed himself to that week. To be fair, a lot of the time Ben did it to himself. He had a talent for doing something completely absurd and then acting confused when it became a running joke for the next three years.

This chapter is basically the story of that talent.

## 51 Monroe

From middle school through freshman year, Ben was obsessed with climbing 51 Monroe.

He would constantly talk about getting onto the roof. He supposedly snuck into the building multiple times and made it almost all the way up, only to discover that the final door leading to the roof was locked.

Naturally, Ben began considering alternative methods.

At one point, he told Nick F that he could get through the locked door with a pressure cooker bomb. After that, the entire thing became one of the group’s earliest running jokes. According to us, all Ben needed to reach the top of 51 Monroe was a pressure cooker bomb, an M4, and 11 hostages.

The roof was apparently being defended like a military installation.

Around this same period, Ben had an old iPhone SE that was practically unusable. The battery would legitimately die after around thirty minutes, the phone was painfully slow, and Ben was constantly unable to use it when he actually needed it.

That phone somehow lasted much longer than it had any right to.

## Quarantine

During quarantine, Ben’s parents decided that he was staying up too late on his computer. Their solution was to block his computer’s MAC address from accessing the internet after around 8:00 p.m.

Ben’s solution was to set up a Raspberry Pi and attempt to route his internet connection through it.

That was basically how quarantine worked for Ben. His parents would create a restriction, and Ben would respond like he was trying to bypass government surveillance.

At night, Ben would pretend to go to sleep so that he could stay up playing Rust with Jordan, Marco, and Eric. He would get ready for bed, lock his bedroom door, turn everything off, and then quietly return to his computer.

At least, he was supposed to be quiet.

One night, Ben was apparently being too loud. His dad, Michael, woke up and began coming toward his room. Ben started panicking in the Discord call and suddenly announced:

**“MICHEAL IS PICKING MY LOCK.”**

He then had to abandon everything, jump into bed, and pretend to be asleep before Michael got inside.

The spelling of “MICHEAL” is important. That was how the quote became remembered.

Ben also had a strange nightly ritual that nobody ever understood. At around 7:00 or 8:00 p.m., he would tell everyone that he had to “get ready for bed.”

Getting ready for bed would somehow take an entire hour.

Nobody knew what he was doing. He would simply disappear for an hour, complete whatever mysterious process was required, and then return to Discord afterward.

Another memorable quarantine incident happened while Ben had his camera turned on during a Discord call. His parents, Michael and Yvonne, entered his room to speak with him. Ben muted himself and apparently believed that he had turned everything off.

He had not turned off his camera.

Everyone in the call silently watched as Michael sat on Ben’s bed and talked to him alongside Yvonne. Ben had no idea that the entire interaction was still being broadcast.

It was not even especially embarrassing. It was just another bizarre piece of Ben footage that the group had unintentionally witnessed.

## The Summer Before Junior Year

The summer after sophomore year was when several parts of Ben’s identity began merging together.

There were the Brooks.

There were the wife beaters.

There was Formula One.

And there were the Ocean City girls.

### The Brooks

Ben had a pair of Brooks running shoes that he wore constantly. Eventually, the shoes developed a reputation within the group for smelling terrible.

Despite this reputation, Ben refused to replace them.

He continued wearing the same Brooks until around the end of junior year. By that point, they were no longer simply shoes. They were an established part of Ben’s character.

Ben was also known for wearing wife beaters. The Brooks and the wife beaters became the unofficial Ben uniform.

That was simply how Ben appeared.

### Ocean City

That summer, the group went to Ocean City and met three girls: Sophie, Linden, and Cara. They eventually became known collectively as the Ocean City girls.

At some point during the trip, Ben apparently decided that the best way to impress them was to drink toilet water.

He drank it and later got sick.

There was no deeper strategy behind this. Ben drank toilet water in front of girls and then suffered the predictable consequences.

### G-Force Training

Ben had always been deeply interested in Formula One, but by around freshman or sophomore year, he began acting as though he might seriously pursue becoming an F1 driver.

This led to what Ben considered G-force training.

He would strap resistance bands around his head or neck and perform exercises intended to strengthen his neck muscles. In theory, this was similar to the type of training professional racing drivers use to withstand high lateral forces.

In practice, it was Ben standing around with resistance bands attached to his head.

Everyone made fun of him because he looked completely ridiculous.

This did not discourage him. Ben was committed to the dream.

## Junior Year

Junior year was the true gold mine.

By then, the Brooks were infamous, the wife beaters were established, and Ben had fully entered an era of romantic schemes, racing ambitions, strange lunches, and public humiliation.

### The Colleen Arc

At the beginning of junior year, Ben began using Snapchat Quick Add to add random girls.

Eventually, he added a girl named Colleen, who was Megan’s sister. She was around one or two years older than us and was known for getting around.

Ben began snapping her.

Colleen was clearly not interested. She would take forever to respond, and when she finally did respond, her messages were extremely dry.

Ben, however, treated every interaction as though it required strategic analysis.

He constantly panicked and overthought what to send her. Sean and Jordan heard about it almost every day. Ben would ask what a particular snap meant, how long he should wait before responding, what angle he should use, and what he should send next.

Colleen could send Ben half of her ceiling with no caption, and Ben would conduct a full investigation into its meaning.

One night after a football game, everyone was at Mahargo’s house playing pool. Somebody told Ben:

**“Hit this pool ball for Colleen.”**

Ben attempted the shot.

He failed so badly that the pool cue passed directly over the ball without touching it.

Colleen had not witnessed this, but somehow the failure still felt romantic.

### “Who Invited This Kid?”

During junior year, the group attended a Halloween party.

The party itself was fun, but at one point, a guy named Nick Cox saw Ben and asked somebody:

**“Who invited this kid?”**

He was referring directly to Ben.

He genuinely wanted to know who had brought Ben to the party, which naturally ensured that the quote would be repeated long after the party ended.

### Parents’ Alc

Another incident occurred while Ben was home alone and his parents were gone.

Ben stole some of their alcohol and drank it by himself. At some point during the night, he texted Jordan:

**“Dude, I’m so drunk.”**

The alcohol became known as **“parents’ alc.”**

The fact that Ben had stolen his parents’ alcohol, gotten drunk alone at home, and then proudly reported the situation to Jordan gave everyone another reason to make fun of him.

### Maria Brooke

One of the biggest incidents of junior year was Maria Brooke.

Eric created a fake Instagram account under the name Maria Brooke and used it to catfish Ben while the rest of the group watched the situation unfold.

Ben believed Maria was real. He became invested enough in the conversation that he almost skipped track practice to meet her and attend her supposed volleyball game.

The full incident is covered in the separate **Maria Brooke** chapter, but it deserves to be mentioned here because it became one of the defining Ben stories from that year.

## Ben’s Formula One Career

Ben did not merely enjoy Formula One. He believed that he could somehow make it into Formula One.

Everyone repeatedly told him that he was delusional. By the time Ben was seriously discussing this plan, he was already far older than most professional drivers had been when they began racing competitively.

Ben remained confident.

He told Sean that he planned to get a job at a racing facility that was located extremely far away, possibly somewhere in West Virginia. When asked how he would live there, Ben proposed sleeping underneath a bridge so that he could work at the facility and use the job as his entry into racing.

He also explained that if he worked for the entire summer, he could save enough money to purchase racing equipment.

Not a go-kart.

The suit for the go-kart.

Ben was prepared to sleep under a bridge for a summer so that he could afford the clothing required for a vehicle he did not own.

Whenever people challenged the plan, Ben described the future lifestyle that racing would provide. He claimed that he would eventually have women on yachts and women in Dubai.

From that point forward, **“Dubai bitches”** became one of the group’s most important catchphrases.

Whenever Ben discussed racing, success, money, or the future, someone would bring up the Dubai bitches awaiting him at the end of his Formula One career.

### The Brown Bag

To save money for his future racing career, Ben stopped buying food while everyone was out.

Instead, he would bring his lunch inside a brown plastic grocery bag. It usually contained something simple, such as a peanut butter and jelly sandwich.

This was not even especially unreasonable. Saving money and bringing lunch from home made sense.

Unfortunately, Ben was already Ben, so people still made fun of him for carrying around the brown bag.

Then he brought the sippy cup.

### The Sippy Cup

One day, Ben arrived at school carrying a children’s sippy cup.

Nobody remembers exactly what was inside it. It might have been apple juice.

The contents were not important.

What mattered was that Ben, a high school junior, had willingly brought a sippy cup to school and begun drinking from it in front of everyone.

The group gave him absolute hell.

The incident contributed to Jordan and Matthew creating one of the most important artifacts of junior year: a classroom whiteboard divided into two categories.

**Things Ben Can Do**

and

**Things Ben Can’t Do**

Both lists existed entirely to make fun of him.

The board remained in the classroom for around a week. Random students and teachers could walk into the room, see an enormous collection of unexplained rules about someone named Ben, and have absolutely no idea what any of it meant.

The group understood, though.

It was the golden age of culture.

## Senior Year

By senior year, Ben had moved beyond strange social decisions and begun experimenting with electricity.

### The Car Battery

Ben would bring a car battery into one of his engineering or technology classes.

He would intentionally short the terminals to create sparks. He also experimented with passing current through pencil lead or graphite, causing it to heat up and glow extremely brightly.

This was apparently just something Ben felt comfortable bringing into a classroom and doing during the school day.

After years of pressure cooker jokes, Raspberry Pi network bypasses, toilet water, resistance-band neck training, and plans to sleep beneath a bridge, Ben had progressed to carrying a car battery around school and making sparks with it.

It was a natural evolution.

### The Scenic Overlook

Another memorable senior-year incident involved the scenic overlook off I-270 on the way toward Frederick.

The overlook was basically a small pull-off directly beside the highway. It was supposedly a place where people could stop and look out toward Frederick, although at night the entire area was pitch-black and there was barely anything to see.

Ben would ask Marco to drive him there.

One night, Ben convinced Marco to make the trip. When they arrived, Ben told Marco to turn off the car’s lights.

Then he told him that they should go to sleep.

Ben said that he did not want to return home because of Michael and everything happening there.

So Marco and Ben sat together at a dark highway overlook, with the lights off, while Ben considered simply not going back.

It was one of the stranger Ben stories, but unlike most of the others, it was not entirely funny. Beneath all the jokes, restrictions, locked doors, and attempts to avoid going home, there was clearly something real happening.

That was part of Ben’s life too.

## Ben’s Life

Ben spent high school trying to climb buildings, bypass internet restrictions, stay awake without his parents noticing, impress girls, become a Formula One driver, save money with brown-bag lunches, drink from sippy cups, and experiment with car batteries.

He drank toilet water.

He nearly skipped track practice for a fake Instagram account.

He performed G-force training with resistance bands around his neck.

He planned to sleep beneath a bridge for a racing job that might eventually allow him to afford a racing suit.

He wanted Dubai bitches.

And through all of it, everyone made fun of him.

Sometimes he deserved it. Sometimes the group took it too far. Most of the time, nobody stopped long enough to tell the difference.

Ben was the punching bag because Ben always gave the group something new to talk about. Every few weeks, another quote, outfit, plan, or disaster entered the culture and became permanent.

“MICHEAL IS PICKING MY LOCK.”

“Who invited this kid?”

“Dude, I’m so drunk.”

Dubai bitches.

Things Ben can do.

Things Ben can’t do.

For better or worse, that was Ben’s life.
