# Ben’s Life — Mechanic Design

## Chosen Concept: Ben’s Greatest Hits

The chapter is a **collection of short, story-specific minigames**, connected by walking scenes, dialogue, and group narration so it still feels like one continuous recollection rather than an arcade compilation.

The whiteboard is one memorable minigame among several. The Formula One sequence is the comic climax, but the chapter covers the full original Ben era from 51 Monroe through the scenic overlook.

## Core Design Rules

- No forced retries.
- Winning and losing both continue the memory.
- Failure should often produce the funnier version.
- No global score, upgrade system, or deep emotional framing.
- Each minigame should recreate something Ben actually did.
- Dialogue, recognizable objects, and original quotes are more important than difficulty.
- Minigames should begin naturally inside each memory rather than through generic “START GAME” screens.
- Most minigames should last between 20 and 60 seconds.
- The Formula One climax may last around 90 to 120 seconds.
- Walking, inspection, dialogue, and short scripted scenes should connect the games and preserve the nostalgic anthology feeling.

# Chapter Flow

## Act I — Early Ben

### Minigame 1: Roof Access Planner

#### Memory

Ben reaches the final roof-access door inside 51 Monroe and discovers that it is locked.

#### Loop

The screen shows the ordinary locked door and a collection of possible tools. The player drags three items into Ben’s proposed roof-access operation.

Possible items include:

- Screwdriver
- Spare key
- Crowbar
- Pressure cooker
- M4
- Eleven hostages

Reasonable tools are rejected.

> **BEN:** “That’s not gonna work, dude.”

The ridiculous items remain attached to the plan.

#### Successful Outcome

The final plan reads:

> **PRESSURE COOKER + M4 + 11 HOSTAGES**

The camera returns to the completely ordinary locked door.

It remains locked.

#### Timeout Outcome

Ben automatically fills in the missing items himself.

#### Duration

Approximately 30 seconds.

#### Purpose

The original joke was Ben escalating a locked door into a military operation. The player physically assembles that escalation rather than completing a generic lockpicking challenge.

---

### Recurring Environmental Gag: The iPhone SE

Ben’s dying iPhone SE appears throughout the early memories.

Whenever he needs it:

- The battery is already red.
- The phone freezes.
- The screen shuts off halfway through something important.
- Ben insists that it was charged.

This is not a full minigame. It becomes a recurring expectation and part of Ben’s equipment lore.

## Act II — Quarantine Ben

### Minigame 2: MICHAEL IS PICKING MY LOCK

#### Memory

Ben finishes his fake-bedtime procedure, returns to Rust, becomes too loud, and realizes Michael is approaching his locked bedroom.

#### Loop

The player sees a simplified Rust and Discord interface.

Three meters appear:

- **Rust Progress**
- **Ben’s Volume**
- **Michael Proximity**

The player continues performing loud Rust actions while attempting to manage Ben’s volume.

Actions may include:

- Mining
- Shooting
- Shouting callouts
- Muting the microphone
- Pretending nothing happened

Eventually, footsteps appear in the hallway and the door handle begins moving.

The final rapid sequence is:

1. Mute microphone.
2. Shut off monitor.
3. Throw headset.
4. Dive into bed.

During the sequence, Ben announces:

> **“MICHAEL IS PICKING MY LOCK.”**

#### Successful Outcome

Ben reaches the bed before the door opens.

Michael enters and sees Ben performing an extremely unconvincing sleeping pose.

#### Failed Outcome

Michael enters while Ben is halfway between the computer and the bed.

Ben freezes as though remaining motionless will solve the problem.

#### Duration

Approximately 50 to 60 seconds.

---

### Follow-Up Vignette: Camera Left On

On another Discord call, Ben mutes himself but leaves his camera active while Michael and Yvonne speak with him.

The player can:

- Tell Ben.
- Say nothing.
- Ask whether everyone else can see this.

The group watches for slightly too long regardless of the choice.

The selection changes the group’s reactions but does not change the remembered event.

## Act III — Ben Becomes Ben

### Presentation Scene: The Uniform

Ben enters wearing the Brooks and wife beater.

The narration pauses and presents them like legendary equipment:

> **THE BROOKS**  
> Condition: Unrecoverable

> **THE WIFE BEATER**  
> Quantity owned: Apparently one

No minigame is needed. The presentation itself is the joke.

---

### Minigame 3: Patient Zero

#### Memory

At Ocean City, Ben drinks toilet water through a straw and later becomes seriously ill.

#### Opening

The drinking itself remains scripted.

The player can:

- Inspect the straw.
- Look at the toilet.
- Ask whether Ben is serious.
- Warn the Ocean City girls.
- Step backward.

Ben drinks regardless.

#### Loop

Eric moves around the Ocean City room while Ben generates increasingly large cough zones.

The player avoids:

- Cough clouds
- Used tissues
- Contaminated objects
- Group members who have acquired sick-status icons

Every few seconds, another person becomes sick and makes the room harder to navigate.

The HUD reads:

> **TIME UNTIL ERIC GETS SICK**

The game does not ask whether Eric becomes sick. It only measures how long he lasts.

#### Strong Performance

Eric survives the immediate scene.

A later montage reveals that he still became sick several days afterward.

> **SURVIVED OCEAN CITY**  
> **DID NOT SURVIVE THE FOLLOWING WEEK**

#### Poor Performance

Eric is hit by the first major cough cloud.

> **PATIENT: IMMEDIATE**

#### Final Stamp

> **TRANSMISSION CHAIN: COMPLETELY UNVERIFIED**  
> **BLAME ASSIGNED TO BEN ANYWAY**

#### Duration

Approximately 35 to 45 seconds.

---

### Short Interactive Scene: G-Force Training

Ben demonstrates resistance-band neck exercises.

The player can:

- Walk around Ben.
- Inspect the bands.
- Ask what muscle this is supposed to train.
- Watch the group react.

Ben continues exercising regardless of the dialogue.

This remains a short visual scene rather than a full challenge.

## Act IV — The Golden Age

### Minigame 4: The Colleen Coaching Simulator

This combines Ben’s Snapchat analysis and the pool-table incident into one two-phase minigame.

---

### Phase One: Analyze the Snap

Ben receives an extremely dry snap from Colleen.

The player advises him on:

- Reply delay
- Camera angle
- Facial expression
- Caption
- Percentage of face visible

The interface treats every decision like a serious tactical operation.

No combination produces a meaningfully interested response.

Possible replies from Colleen include:

- Ceiling
- Forehead
- Dark room
- Half an eye
- Empty black screen

The best possible result is:

> **2% MORE FACE THAN LAST TIME**

---

### Phase Two: Hit It for Colleen

The scene transitions to Mahargo’s pool table.

Someone says:

> “Hit this pool ball for Colleen.”

The camera becomes dramatically cinematic. Music swells. The player aligns the cue and presses to shoot.

The cue passes over the ball.

The player’s timing only determines how dramatically Ben misses:

- Slightly above the ball
- Entirely above the table
- Cue nearly leaves Ben’s hands

The ball never moves.

#### Duration

Approximately 60 seconds total.

#### Purpose

The player participates in Ben’s excessive analysis, but the outcomes remain faithful: Colleen stays uninterested and Ben misses the ball.

---

### Minigame 5: CAN BEN…?

#### Memory

Jordan and Matthew create the classroom whiteboard:

- **Things Ben Can Do**
- **Things Ben Can’t Do**

#### Existing Mode

Use the existing configurable `benTrivia` mode.

The player slam-sorts canonical entries between the two categories.

The existing whiteboard minigame remains authoritative for every entry and should not be reconstructed from memory.

#### Environmental Presentation

While the player sorts:

- Unrelated students walk past.
- Teachers pause to read the board.
- Nobody receives context.
- The board becomes increasingly official-looking.
- Jordan and Matthew argue over classifications.
- The board remains visible for an absurd amount of time.

#### Final Choice

After sorting, the player chooses:

1. Add another accurate entry.
2. Defend one thing Ben can genuinely do.
3. Send the photograph to Ben.

The choice changes immediate dialogue but does not create a separate chapter ending.

#### Duration

Approximately 75 to 90 seconds.

#### Importance

This is the chapter’s second-largest minigame and the major interactive centerpiece of the junior-year section.

---

### Minigame 6: The Bridge-Born Champion

#### Memory

Sean recounts Ben’s complete Formula One career plan.

#### Role

This is the chapter’s comic climax and longest minigame.

It is an interactive retelling rather than a boss fight. The player’s objective is to uncover the complete plan, not defeat Ben.

#### Core Loop

The player chooses which question to ask next:

- How do you become a driver?
- Where is the facility?
- How much does the job pay?
- Where will you live?
- What can you afford?
- Do you own a kart?
- When does Dubai happen?

Every question expands the visual scene and reveals another layer of Ben’s logic.

---

### Phase One: G-Force Training

Ben appears with resistance bands around his neck.

The player briefly keeps a balance marker centered while the bands pull Ben’s head in different directions.

Whether the player succeeds or fails, Ben declares himself prepared for Formula One.

---

### Phase Two: Employment

A large counter displays:

> **$15.00 / HOUR**

The player allocates Ben’s earnings between:

- Food
- Housing
- Transportation
- Kart
- Racing suit

There is not enough money for everything.

Ben removes housing from the budget.

---

### Phase Three: Bridge Housing

A bridge drops into the scene.

The player places Ben’s sleeping bag beneath it while avoiding:

- Highway runoff
- Passing traffic effects
- Poor sleeping locations
- Obviously unusable terrain

Once placed:

> **HOUSING PROBLEM: SOLVED**

---

### Phase Four: Equipment

The player opens Ben’s purchased racing equipment.

It contains:

- Racing suit
- Gloves
- Possibly a helmet
- No kart

Ben insists:

> “The kart comes later.”

---

### Phase Five: Dubai

The player asks how any of this leads to professional racing.

The background suddenly becomes Dubai.

A yacht enters from the side.

Ben delivers:

> “Bitches on yachts.”

The game records:

> **DUBAI BITCHES ADDED TO GROUP VOCABULARY**

#### Successful Outcome

The player uncovers every stage of the plan.

> **CAREER PLAN: FULLY DOCUMENTED**  
> **VIABILITY: NOT ASSESSED**

#### Timeout Outcome

Ben skips the remaining logistical details and goes directly to Dubai.

> **LOGISTICAL DETAILS OMITTED**

#### Duration

Approximately 90 to 120 seconds.

#### Purpose

The player is rewarded for revealing more of the story. The escalating background allows the minigame to preserve the feeling of friends asking questions and discovering that Ben has already produced an even stranger answer.

## Act V — Senior Ben

### Minigame 7: Portable Electrical Hazard

#### Memory

Ben brings a full-sized car battery to school and experiments with it under the supervision of an indifferent long-term substitute.

#### Loop

Objects appear on the classroom workbench:

- Car battery
- Battery terminals
- Wires or clips
- Pencil lead
- Graphite
- Ordinary classroom objects

The player connects the correct components through escalating stages:

1. Reveal the battery.
2. Short the terminals.
3. Create sparks.
4. Connect the graphite.
5. Hold the connection inside the glowing zone.
6. Produce smoke.

A large meter shows:

> **SUBSTITUTE ATTENTION**

It never rises above zero.

Even when sparks and smoke appear, the substitute remains at the desk and does nothing.

#### Successful Outcome

The graphite becomes intensely bright and begins smoking.

> **EXPERIMENT COMPLETE**  
> **ADULT INTERVENTION: NONE**

#### Failed Outcome

The graphite snaps before reaching full brightness.

Ben immediately pulls out another pencil and repeats the experiment himself.

The substitute still does nothing.

#### Duration

Approximately 40 to 50 seconds.

#### Purpose

This is the absurd physical endpoint of Ben’s equipment progression:

> iPhone → Raspberry Pi → sippy cup → racing equipment → car battery

It acts as the final exhibit before the chapter’s coda.

---

## Coda — The Scenic Overlook

There is no minigame.

Because Eric was not present, the sequence is framed clearly as Maharko recounting the memory.

The scene shows:

- Maharko and Ben arriving at the overlook
- The vehicle’s lights turning off
- Ben saying, “Dude, dude, just go to sleep.”
- Ben saying that he cannot go home
- Maharko feeling unusually uncomfortable
- A period of silence
- Maharko eventually driving Ben home

The scene remains brief and observational.

It does not:

- Explain Ben psychologically
- Give the player a rescue choice
- Turn the chapter into a tragedy
- Retroactively justify every earlier incident

The narration returns to the larger timeline and closes with:

> **Ben drifted from the group. The Dubai bitches are still waiting.**

# Implementation Strategy

## Recommended Mode Count

The chapter should not require seven completely separate mode implementations.

A practical structure is:

### Existing Mode

- `benTrivia`
  - Used for the whiteboard.

### New Reusable Short-Memory Mode

A configurable mode for brief one-screen or one-room interactions:

- Roof Access Planner
- Colleen Snap Analysis
- Formula One budgeting and item-selection phases
- Car Battery wiring

Possible mode ID:

```typescript
benMemoryGame
```

The mode config determines:

- Prompt type
- Draggable or selectable objects
- Timer
- Required sequence
- Scripted success result
- Scripted failure result
- Dialogue barks
- Completion delay

### New Movement Hazard Mode

A reusable movement-and-avoidance structure for:

- Patient Zero
- Possibly the Rust panic sequence with specialized configuration

Possible mode ID:

```typescript
benHazard
```

Configurable elements:

- Arena bounds
- Hazard spawn rate
- Survival timer
- Escalation steps
- Player movement speed
- Success and failure labels
- Whether failure ends immediately or merely changes the final joke

### Custom Formula One Mode

The Formula One climax may justify its own mode because it contains multiple presentation phases and serves as the chapter’s primary comic peak.

Possible mode ID:

```typescript
bridgeBornChampion
```

### Scripted Story Beats

Use normal story beats for:

- iPhone SE failures
- The uniform reveal
- Camera left on
- G-force presentation
- Halloween party
- Parents’ alc
- “I’m hip”
- Maria Brooke callback
- Brown bag
- Sippy cup
- Scenic overlook

This keeps the chapter varied without creating unnecessary implementation bloat.

# Difficulty Philosophy

The chapter should be easy on a first attempt.

The goal is to experience the memories and jokes, not master difficult mechanics.

Recommended rules:

- Generous timers
- Clear controls
- Minimal punishment
- No repeated tutorials
- No forced replay
- Losing routes directly back into the story
- Failure should reveal alternate jokes rather than remove content
- The Formula One sequence should always reach Dubai
- The whiteboard should complete even if the player performs poorly
- The player should never miss an important canonical quote because of gameplay failure

---

# Final Chapter Identity

> **A playable roast compilation in which each Ben memory becomes a short interactive joke, and every new story somehow makes the previous one seem reasonable.**

The chapter should feel like the group is sitting together remembering Ben:

> “Wait, do you remember when he did this?”

The memory becomes playable, ends on its strongest punchline, and transitions immediately into the next story.
