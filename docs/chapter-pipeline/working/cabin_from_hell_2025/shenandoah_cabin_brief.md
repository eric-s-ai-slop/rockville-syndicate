# Project Omega: The Rockville Syndicate
## Creative Brief — Chapter Extraction (Step 1)
### Summer 2025 — Ocean City & Shenandoah Cabin

---

## Logline

Summer 2025. Eight friends outgrow Ocean City, flee to a one-bathroom cabin in Shenandoah, and spend four days losing a war they didn't all agree to fight — a disease Maharko brought home from a balcony kiss spreading body by body while Eric and Alex escalate a nightly speaker prank room by room, both ladders converging on a cabin that's been failing since they arrived. They leave a day early. The cabin wins.

---

## Structure

**Siege / anthology.** Two acts physically, but the cabin runs as a continuous siege — Day 2 through Day 5, each day adding to what's already broken. The comedy is accumulation: *meanwhile, the water is still out.*

### Act 1 — Ocean City
Day 0, ~2 nights. Plants: the disease (Maharko's kiss), the "too old for OC" exit, Benji's "I Love My Girlfriend" shirt and cig summer texture, the H2O letdown, Maharko down bad.

### Act 2 — The Siege
Days 1–5, single location (Shenandoah cabin). Runs continuously. Day cuts are narrator beats, not scene changes. The cabin is the third combatant — indifferent failure that compounds whoever is already losing.

### Spines (two, mirrored)

- **Ladder A — Contagion.** Accidental, person-to-person, monotonic. Maharko → Jordan → Nick F → Alex & Leo. Seeded in Act 1 (the kiss). Spreads through shared air, shared bed, shared bong, the one bathroom. Nobody recovers.
- **Ladder B — Prank War.** Engineered, room-to-room, nightly. Eric & Alex. Rung 1: hidden speaker, found, doors barricaded. Rung 2: speaker under pullout mattress + corner speaker, doors still barricaded. Rung 3: speaker locked in bathroom, fork-unlock weaponized. Seeded in Act 2 (the bedroom claim the kidney stones made possible).
- **The mirror, named out loud:** Both escalate nightly. Both are unstoppable once started. Both are seeded before Day 2. The illness spreads through proximity; the pranks spread through hidden objects in proximity. One is a body failing; the other is a body escalating. The cabin is the third combatant.
- **The fusion point (locked):** Day 5, Alex gets sick. The contagion ladder eats the prank ladder's operator. The two spines fuse. Retreat follows.

## Locations & Who's Present

### Act 1 — Ocean City
Condo/hotel with a balcony, the bars, H2O (the under-21 club they've outgrown). All eight: Eric, Nick H, Nick F, Jordan, Leo, Alex, Benji, Maharko. Plus the two balcony girls (guest).

### Act 2 — Shenandoah cabin
One bedroom (Eric + Alex), one bedroom (Maharko + Jordan, the sick room), living room with pullout (Nick H, Nick F, Leo, Benji), one shared bathroom, deck (lanternflies), surrounding trails (the hike). The host is offscreen (one phone call).

## Emotional Arc

- **Start:** "We're young, this is our summer, OC is ours." Confidence. Cig summer energy. The bars work. The fakes work. Maharko's down bad but that's just Maharko being Maharko.
- **Turn:** Day 2. The water is out. Maharko is sick. The deck is full of bugs. The cabin is not what they booked. The confidence becomes confusion — not crisis yet, just "why is this happening."
- **End:** Day 5. Alex and Leo are sick. The group decides to leave early. The feeling is not defeat exactly — it's recognition. The cabin was never going to let them win. The retreat is clean, not dramatic. They pack up. They go. The cabin stays.

---

## Chapter Identity

A siege where the generals keep winning battles and still lose the war — because the war was never against them, it was against the building. A nature documentary where the subject is a cabin, and the friends are the invasive species the cabin is quietly rejecting.

> *Cabin fever as siege warfare — Eric and Alex as the engineering corps, Maharko's balcony kiss as the bioweapon nobody deployed on purpose.*

## The Player's Position

The player is **the group** — a collective POV managed through the narrator ("The Group Chat"), same as canon. But the player's sympathy shifts across the chapter:

- **Act 1:** with Maharko (down bad, makes the call) and Benji (the witness).
- **Act 2 Days 2–3:** with the living room (the victims of both ladders — they're getting sick AND getting pranked).
- **Act 2 Nights 1–3:** with Eric and Alex (the engineers — the comedy is the engineering).
- **Act 2 Day 5:** with the group as a whole (the retreat). No one is winning.

The player is never asked to take a side permanently. The siege structure lets the player be the prankster and the victim in different acts. This is intentional — it's how the mirror lands. You can't be on Eric's side during Rung 3 and not feel the turn when Alex gets sick the next morning.

## The Choices

Siege/anthology: up to one choice per act, one must be the chapter's biggest. Two acts → up to two choices. Each must be a real fork with real cost.

### Choice 1 — Act 1 (the biggest): The Balcony

Maharko is down bad. Benji has opened the conversation. The two girls are upstairs. The non-chopped girl is right there.

- **Option A: Kiss the chopped girl.** She's there, she's willing, you're down bad. *Cost: the disease. The whole siege is the cost of this kiss.*
- **Option B: Hold out for the non-chopped girl.** She's not into you, but maybe. *Cost: dignity, maybe pride; the siege doesn't happen, but neither does the story.*
- **Option C: Don't go up at all.** Benji's wearing the shirt. Benji didn't go up to hook up. *Cost: Maharko stays down bad; the trip is just a cabin trip.*

*This is the chapter's biggest choice — it seeds Ladder A. Every sick body downstream is the cost of Option A. The game telegraphs this lightly: the narrator notes the non-chopped girl is right there, and notes that Benji (in the shirt) opened the door and didn't walk through it.*

### Choice 2 — Act 2, Night 3 (the peak of Ladder B): The Bathroom Prank

Eric has discovered the fork-unlock. The bathroom is the one room the living room people can't easily enter. The plan is ready.

- **Option A: Lock the speaker in the bathroom.** The peak. The fork is the weapon. *Cost: this is the rung that makes the living room people hate you — and 36 hours later, Alex is sick, and the operator becomes the casualty. The cabin fuses the ladders on the back of this rung.*
- **Option B: Skip the rung.** The Night 2 setup was enough. Don't escalate to the bathroom. *Cost: the peak of Ladder B doesn't happen; the chapter loses its mirror-collision.*
- **Option C: Hide the speaker in the sick room instead.** Turn the prank on Maharko and Jordan. *Cost: you're attacking the infected — funny, but cruel; changes the read of Eric and Alex from engineers to opportunists.*

*This choice seeds the fusion. Option A is canon. The cost of A is paid on Day 5.*

## The Boss

**The cabin itself.** Not a person. Not Maharko (he's bed-bound by Day 2). Not the disease (it's a condition, not an antagonist). The boss is the cabin — the building that has been failing since they arrived, the environment that compounds whoever is already losing, the third combatant that doesn't take sides and doesn't stop.

**What the player is fighting for:** not victory — *survival of the trip.* The trip is the thing the cabin is rejecting. The player is trying to get through the planned duration with the group intact. They can't. The cabin rejects the group.

**What winning feels like:** there is no winning. The mechanical win condition is *enduring to the planned end date*. The emotional win condition is the group staying together through it. The chapter denies both.

**What losing feels like:** the retreat. Day 5. The group decides together to leave. The cabin stays. The feeling is recognition, not defeat — the cabin was always going to win. The player has been fighting the building the whole time and didn't fully know it.

**The fight's peak (mechanic agent's call):** Night 3 is the playable peak — the bathroom door, the fork, the 15 minutes. This is where Ladder B peaks. But the *boss* is the cabin, which means the cabin's boss moment is Day 5: the morning Alex and Leo are sick, the group makes the call. The mechanic agent should decide whether the boss is a single encounter (Night 3 bathroom as the playable peak, with the retreat as narrator denouement) or a siege-mode encounter across Day 5 (the cabin's final assault — AC, water, bugs, illness all stacked).

*Read: Night 3 is the playable peak of Ladder B (the prank war), but the boss — the cabin — is encountered on Day 5 as a siege the player can't win, only survive long enough to retreat. The retreat is the boss resolution. The cabin wins; the player's win condition is "get out together."*

## The Real Subject

- **Surface:** a cabin trip goes wrong.
- **Real:** the gap between engineering a situation and inheriting one. Eric and Alex engineer; Maharko inherits; the cabin is the third thing — indifferent failure. The engineers lose because the inheritance (disease) and the indifference (cabin) are not problems you can engineer around. The control was real and it didn't matter.

## The Question the Story Asks

**Can you be in control and still lose?** Yes. And the loss is worse because you were.

## Hinge Character Profile — Leo

Leo. His kidney stones delay Car 2, which creates the bedroom-claim-in-absentia, which creates the room geometry the whole siege runs on. Without Leo's body failing, the room war has no structure and the prank war has no rooms to escalate through. He's the accidental architect.

His relationship to the group: the Casualty — "if something medical, statistical, or cosmic is going to happen to exactly one member of the group, it happens to Leo." This trip is an early instance (or origin) of that status. He reports his own disasters with eerie calm.

What he knows at the moment he acts: nothing. He doesn't choose to have kidney stones. He's in the ER. He's the hinge because his body fails on a schedule the group can't control, and the delay his failure creates is what lets Eric and Alex claim the bedroom before he arrives.

Why he does what he does: he doesn't. That's the point. The hinge is passive. His body makes the decision.

What it costs him: the bedroom. He ends up in the living room — the room that gets cooked, the room that gets the speakers, the room that gets everything. Leo's kidney stones cost him the war. And then on Day 5, the cabin gets him too — the Casualty's arc completes. He came in with his body failing; he leaves with the cabin having finished what his body started.

Afterward: he reports the kidney stones with eerie calm. The cabin illness is just another data point. "So we stopped at urgent care. It's kidney stones." Then, later: "So we left a day early. The cabin won." Same voice. The Casualty defends his status.

## Guest Character Profiles

### The chopped girl (patient zero)
Super drunk, "fat and chopped," on the balcony one floor up. Not obviously sick. Willing to make out with Maharko. She is the seed of Ladder A. She does not know she's sick (or if she does, she doesn't care — she's super drunk). Default move under pressure: she's not under pressure; she's the pressure. Speech pattern: drunk, sloppy, willing. What's underneath: nothing the chapter needs. She's a vector, not a character. The chapter's cruelty is that she's a person reduced to a vector — and the group will spend four days paying for that reduction without ever naming it.

### The non-chopped girl
Right there. Not into Maharko (likely because he chose the chopped girl). The road not taken. Default move: judgment — the non-reaction that is a reaction. Speech pattern: brief, dry. What's underneath: she's the alternative the chapter offers and Maharko refuses. Option B in Choice 1 walks through the door she's holding open.

### The cabin host
Offscreen. One phone call. "Just wait." Water returns. The indifferent universe, personified. Not malicious — absent. Default move: tell you to wait. Speech pattern: customer-service flat. What's underneath: nothing. The host is the cabin's human face, and the face is a phone tree.

## Artifact Description — The "I Love My Girlfriend" Shirt

A shirt Benji wears constantly. Jordan and Maharko roast him for it in Act 1. By Act 5, the shirt is a verdict they didn't know they were issuing.

What made it work: it's both a joke (the roasting) and a position (Benji's actual commitment). The shirt is the visible surface of Benji's anchor — his girlfriend, offstage, the reason the contagion ladder can't have him and the reason the prank war doesn't land on him. He's the Constant because he's already claimed.

When it stopped being just a shirt: Day 5. Maharko bed-bound, Jordan bed-bound, Nick F sick, Alex sick, Leo sick — and Benji is in the living room, smoking, in the shirt, unchanged. The shirt was right. The roasting was wrong. The shirt is the chapter's quiet moral center, and the moral is: *decide what you're about, and the cabin can't touch you.* Benji decided. Nobody else did.
